## Current

### Metrics and logs

- **Concurrency waiting reasons name the limit and value** (D91): A waiting reason names the reached
  concurrency limit and its value. `schedr_tasks_waiting` labels these waits by the limit’s name.
- **The UI shows watcher hosts and switched-off sensors** (D343): The Workers page shows each host’s
  code hash, wait steps, threads, memory and losses; the metrics give totals over the hosts
  (`worker_watcher_hosts_lost` by code). The DAG page lists switched-off sensors with switch-on; the
  DAGs list shows their count.
- **No OpenTelemetry** (D386): schedr exposes Prometheus metrics and logs; it has no OpenTelemetry
  (no OTLP push, no traces across components).
- **Wait steps aren't isolated after host losses** (D387): A lost watcher host blames none of its
  wait steps, and they aren't moved to processes of their own; `worker_watcher_hosts_lost` shows the
  losses, and this is reconsidered if they show up in real use.

### Operational views

- **Record leader and standby masters with heartbeats** (D123): Each running master heartbeats a
  masters table row with its role and configuration. A master that stops reporting ages out.
- **Show node problems on the Deployments graph** (D129): Warnings colour nodes yellow and errors
  red; panels list current problems. Unreachable webservers are errors and version mismatches
  warnings; masters report current problems in heartbeats. Lost workers are errors, while
  disconnected workers, reported warnings and import errors produce warnings. Offline workers have
  no problems and remain grey and dashed, ignoring stale warnings and import errors. *Not found in
  the code yet:* offline workers are grey.

### Metrics in the UI

- **Masters, workers and the webserver report their memory and CPU** (D463, planned (M5.1h)): Each
  process reports its resident memory and CPU time, and its container's memory now and its limit
  from the cgroup: a master in its masters row, a worker in its heartbeat (a new field, the
  protocol's minor version up), the webserver in its `/deployment` answer. A worker splits its
  memory into the agent itself, its fork servers, its watcher hosts and its running tasks, with its
  largest tasks named. Not chosen: processes only, without the limit or the breakdown; the UI
  scraping each process's `/metrics` (no process records that address, and it gives no history).
- **The master keeps a sample of each process a minute** (D464, planned (M5.1i)): The leading master
  writes one sample per process a minute (memory, CPU, the container's memory and limit, a worker's
  breakdown) into a samples table (a new migration on both databases). Kept a minute apart for 24 h,
  then rolled up into 15-minute averages and peaks kept 30 days; retention deletes older ones, its
  ages hidden values. Not chosen: one a minute for 7 days without a rollup.

### Other

- **Clock and fencing metrics on workers and the master** (D448): Workers export the age of their
  master-time sample, the round trip it was measured over, and a count of streams ended for an older
  lease epoch; the master exports the attempts held for a replaced incarnation. The start-slack and
  `TOO_LATE` metrics went with the start deadline (D460). Not chosen: worker metrics only.
