## Current

### Metrics and logs

- **Concurrency waiting reasons name the limit and value** (D91): A waiting reason names the reached
  concurrency limit and its value. `schedr_tasks_waiting` labels these waits by the limit’s name.
- **The UI shows watcher hosts and switched-off sensors** (D343): The Workers page shows each host’s
  code hash, wait steps, threads, memory and losses; the metrics give totals over the hosts
  (`worker_watcher_hosts_lost` by code). The DAG page lists switched-off sensors with switch-on; the
  DAGs list shows their count.
- **No OpenTelemetry** (D386): schedr exposes Prometheus metrics and logs; it has no OpenTelemetry
  (no OTLP push, no traces across components).
- **Wait steps aren't isolated after host losses** (D387): A lost watcher host blames none of its
  wait steps, and they aren't moved to processes of their own; `worker_watcher_hosts_lost` shows the
  losses, and this is reconsidered if they show up in real use.

### Operational views

- **Record leader and standby masters with heartbeats** (D123): Each running master heartbeats a
  masters table row with its role and configuration. A master that stops reporting ages out.
- **Show node problems on the Deployments graph** (D129): Warnings colour nodes yellow and errors
  red; panels list current problems. Unreachable webservers are errors and version mismatches
  warnings; masters report current problems in heartbeats. Lost workers are errors, while
  disconnected workers, reported warnings and import errors produce warnings. Offline workers have
  no problems and remain grey and dashed, ignoring stale warnings and import errors. *Not found in
  the code yet:* offline workers are grey.

### Metrics in the UI

- **Masters, workers and the webserver report their memory and CPU** (D463): Each process reports
  its resident memory and CPU time, and its container's memory now and its limit from the cgroup: a
  master in its masters row, a worker in its heartbeat (a new field, the protocol's minor version
  up), the webserver in its `/deployment` answer. A worker splits its memory into the agent itself,
  its fork servers, its watcher hosts and its running tasks, with its largest tasks named. Not
  chosen: processes only, without the limit or the breakdown; the UI scraping each process's
  `/metrics` (no process records that address, and it gives no history).
- **The master keeps a sample of each process a minute** (D464): One sample per process a minute
  (its resident memory and its whole memory with a worker's parts (decision 491), CPU cores since
  the last, the container's memory and limit, a worker's parts and largest tasks) in `samples` (the
  open `0002` migration), keyed by process: the leading master writes its masters' and workers' (a
  standalone one row, its roles master, worker and webserver), a webserver in a process of its own
  its own. Each closed 15-minute window is rolled up into averages and peaks (a worker's parts
  averaged over every minute, its largest tasks left out, the latest limit), each row with the
  `minutes` it covers (and of them with a CPU or container figure, and `covered`: its window's
  minutes sampled at all), by which a bucket's averages and a deployment's totals are weighted (note
  9); minute rows are kept 24 h, rollups 30 d (hidden timings), deleted by the sampler. Not chosen:
  one a minute for 7 days without a rollup. *Corrected by N9:* Codex's review of M5.1j: totals,
  parts and limits made exact; buckets in SQL.
- **The Metrics endpoints keep their final buckets between requests** (D492): `GET
  /api/v1/metrics/samples` and `/memory` keep, per range and process (or all), the buckets that
  ended `FINAL_AFTER` (5 minutes: the sampler's grace, a minute's write, clocks apart) ago, whose
  rows are all stored, with each process's latest sample; a request reads only the buckets since,
  and one for a key waits for one already reading it. At most 64 keys (the least recently used go)
  of 100,000 buckets each. So the Deployments page's sparkline and the Metrics page cost a few
  milliseconds a refresh after their first read.

### Other

- **Clock and fencing metrics on workers and the master** (D448): Workers export the age of their
  master-time sample, the round trip it was measured over, and a count of streams ended for an older
  lease epoch; the master exports the attempts held for a replaced incarnation. The start-slack and
  `TOO_LATE` metrics went with the start deadline (D460). Not chosen: worker metrics only.
